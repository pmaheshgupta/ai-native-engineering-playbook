Why "20–30% productivity" is a trap

Published results range from 56% faster to 19% slower. The spread is the finding: the gain you realize depends on the work you point AI at.

Your realized gain equals your task mix.

More boilerplate, tests and greenfield moves you toward +56%. More complex logic in mature code, which is most fintech production work, moves you toward zero or below.

Action: measure your task mix before believing any headline number.

Engineers cannot self-report productivity.

METR's developers were 19% slower while feeling 20% faster.

Action: never justify AI spend on surveys. Instrument cycle time, escape rate and review load.

Cost, effort and break-even

Does climbing pay off? It depends on team size

About 60% of the L2 build is fixed: the platform, gates and measurement cost the same for 5 engineers or 50. The same build returns ~4 FTE-equivalents a year across 40 engineers and ~0.5 across 5.

Running cost / yr≈1.5 FTE

Running cost = ~1 FTE/yr of upkeep plus the ~1.5 person-year build spread over 3 years. Planning estimate, not a quote.

Why the higher rungs pull away

L1Nothing compoundsA one-time individual speed-up, then flat.
L2Shared prompts, context, legible code, measurementEvery future AI interaction gets better.
L3Redesigned workflows and team skillAgents' output, which depends on good specs, tests and legible code.
L4The system improves itselfCapacity, with compute, and the learning rate.

L1 gains are a step you take once. From L2 up, gains accumulate, so the gap between a compounding org and a flat one widens at an accelerating rate. "We already get 20–30%, that's enough" is a decision to let competitors compound past you.

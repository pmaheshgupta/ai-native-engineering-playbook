Execution guide

From using Claude yourself to leading an AI-native org

The ladder says which rung to aim for. This says what to do, who owns it and where in the SDLC it lands, from lean startups to enterprises. The gap it closes is turning a personal skill into an organizational capability.

Eight concepts that bridge codegen to infrastructure

Agent

Autocomplete finishes a line. An agent completes a task: it reads files, runs commands, edits, checks and iterates. A loop, not a suggestion.

Headless, in the pipeline

Running Claude with no human at the keyboard, such as claude -p in CI or the GitHub Action on every PR. This is the line between L1 and L2.

CLAUDE.md context

A markdown file committed to the repo that Claude reads automatically: conventions, architecture, "don'ts". Tribal knowledge, versioned.

MCP connectors

The Model Context Protocol lets agents read and act on Jira, Drive, Slack and internal APIs instead of you pasting context in.

Policy-as-code

Compliance rules as pipeline checks that block violations, such as "no direct writes to the ledger table". A rule can't be forgotten.

Eval harness

A fixed task set you re-run whenever a prompt or agent changes. Without it, "we improved the prompt" is a feeling, not a fact.

Autonomy policy

An enforced matrix of what agents may do unsupervised, with review, or never, by risk tier. Built before autonomy grows.

Orchestration

One engineer directing several agents in parallel. This is where output starts to scale with agents rather than headcount.

Goal: every engineer using AI productively every day, on approved tools, without leaking data.

The moves

  • Standardize on a sanctioned toolset and end shadow usage on personal accounts. Ungoverned tools are the real risk here.
  • Write the data-handling policy first: which code, data and secrets may go to which AI tools. In fintech this is the entry ticket.
  • Drop a starter CLAUDE.md into your top 5 repos so output is house-consistent from day one.
  • Open an #ai-engineering channel with a weekly 30-minute "what worked, what didn't". Let power users teach.

Startup vs enterprise

Startup (<30 eng)
Done in a week: one policy doc, licences on a card, a channel.
Enterprise (150+)
Tool and data policy must clear InfoSec, Legal and procurement. Start now; that's the long pole. Sanctioning a tool also pulls shadow AI into the light.

What the leader personally does

Sponsor the data policy with Security so it's a fast yes, not a six-month committee. Use Claude publicly so adoption is modeled from the top. Name one owner for AI enablement, even as a 10% role.

Goal: AI woven into shared workflows and CI, with a verification spine and real measurement. The team's throughput moves, not just individuals'.

The moves

  • AI review on 100% of PRs via the GitHub Action or claude -p in CI: bugs, spec conformance and security smells before a human looks.
  • Promote CLAUDE.md to maintained shared context per repo or domain, plus a library of team-standard tasks.
  • Test generation with every change, human-reviewed on risk paths.
  • Policy-as-code gates on money-movement, auth and PII paths. A violating change cannot merge.
  • Instrument the baseline: cycle time p50/p75, review-queue wait, change-failure rate, escape rate.
  • Route sensitive diffs to mandatory human review with CODEOWNERS.

By SDLC stage

PlanCodeTestReviewShipRun
  • Plan: AI-drafted, human-signed specs.
  • Code: scaffold-from-spec as a shared recipe.
  • Test: AI-generated tests and fuzzing as standard.
  • Review: AI first pass on every PR; humans shift to judgment.
  • Ship: policy-as-code gates in the pipeline.
  • Run is usually the next frontier, not needed to claim L2.

Startup vs enterprise

Startup
A 2–3 person platform squad for about two quarters. Favor PR review, CLAUDE.md and policy-as-code over completeness.
Enterprise
The hard part is standardizing across divisions with their own pipelines. Run it as an internal platform product with 2–3 pilot divisions, prove the metrics, then template it.

What the leader personally does

Fund the platform squad and protect it from feature work. Set the rule that separates Augmented from chaos: change-failure rate stays flat while cycle time drops. Take the measured before-and-after uplift to peers and the board. Below ~15 engineers this level often won't pay back; staying at L1 is then the disciplined choice.

Goal: most work starts with AI, agents run multi-step tasks with checkpoints, and workflows are genuinely redesigned around AI.

The moves

  • Redesign one flagship workflow AI-first. The test: a step disappears or fundamentally changes, such as review moving from style nits to spec conformance.
  • Agents on multi-step tasks (ticket → branch → code → tests → PR) with defined human checkpoints, using MCP to read Jira and design docs.
  • Machine-actionable specs an agent can execute without human translation.
  • Update the career ladder to reward AI-leveraged outcomes and verification quality, not hand-written lines of code.

Startup vs enterprise

Startup
No legacy process to unwind, so workflows can be redesigned in weeks. The risk is skipping the L2 verification spine.
Enterprise
Change management, not procurement. Governance, audit expectations and division politics are the real work. Expect quarters, not weeks.

What the leader personally does

This level lives or dies on leadership. Own the operating-model shift and the career-ladder change; no platform team can do that for you. Say it repeatedly: AI-first means humans move up to judgment, not out.

Goal: agents own workflows end to end within enforced guardrails, humans orchestrate and verify, and output scales with agent capacity.

The moves

  • An eval harness that measures AI output quality over time. AI workflows are now managed as products.
  • A published, enforced autonomy policy by risk tier: unsupervised, review-required, human-only.
  • Scoped, least-privilege agent credentials. Never shared human accounts; every action logged and attributable.
  • Redesign the org: fewer, more senior engineers orchestrating agents, QA reframed as verification design, hiring plans that read "hire 2 plus agent capacity".

Run is now included: AI detects and triages incidents, drafts RCAs and remediates known issues within guardrails. Humans keep incident command.

Startup vs enterprise

Startup
A frontier you approach. A small elite team on greenfield systems may genuinely operate here.
Enterprise
A multi-year program. For most, moving decisively through L3 is the honest near-term ambition.

What the leader personally does

Treat it as company strategy, not an engineering initiative. It changes cost structure, hiring and competitive posture, so it belongs in board conversations. The job becomes governing the system: evals, autonomy limits, and where humans must stay.

Non-negotiable at every level

The fintech spine grows ahead of autonomy

These hold at every rung and must be built before agents are allowed to do more, not after.

A named human owner at every regulated gate

Money movement, auth, PII, access and model changes. Codified, tool-enforced and audit-survivable.

Full auditability

Every AI-influenced change is traceable: what AI made, who reviewed it, against which spec.

Least-privilege agent credentials

Agents get their own scoped identities, never shared human service accounts.

Brakes before engine

Automated rollback and progressive delivery come before any increase in autonomy.

For the board: compliance becomes a growth lever.Done right, the spine lowers audit cost, shortens enterprise sales (security review is the usual deal bottleneck) and forms a moat a smaller rival can't quickly copy.

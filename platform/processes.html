Operating processes

As of 2 November 2026 · Mahesh Gupta

Fourteen processes run the platform day to day. Each one scales its approvals to the risk of what is being changed, so a low-risk prompt tweak ships the same day while a change to an agent that moves money waits for risk sign-off. Target times are suggestions to calibrate with your teams.

Risk tiers

TierWhat puts work in this tierExamplesApproval depth
1 LowInternal use, public or internal data, read-only, a person reviews every outputPolicy Q&A assistant, meeting summariserOwner plus automated checks
2 MediumConfidential or personal data, customer-facing content, or reversible actionsSupport reply drafter, ticket-creating agent, sales email agentOwner, peer review, security and data checks
3 HighAutonomous actions with financial, legal or safety effect; decisions about people; EU AI Act high-risk uses; agents called from outside the organisationRefund approver, credit or hiring screener, agent that changes production systemsAll of tier 2, plus Responsible AI lead and CRO sign-off and a change advisory board

The tier is set at use-case intake and recorded in the registry; any change that raises the tier re-runs the approvals for the new tier.

Onboarding a new agent at a glance

Data and security checks run in parallel; only tier 3 agents wait for the risk approval before the release manager's change record.

Onboarding

Onboard a new line of business

  1. Business unit head: requests access and names a sponsor, an AI owner and a cost centre.

  2. Platform product owner: confirms fit, agrees the first use cases and an onboarding date.

  3. FinOps: sets the budget, alert thresholds and showback tags.

  4. DPO and data steward (tier 2+): record which data classes the business will use and any residency needs.

  5. CRO (tier 3, regulated business): confirms sector rules such as RBI or IRDAI and any extra controls.

  6. Platform engineering: creates the tenant: workspace, virtual keys, quotas, single-sign-on groups, default guardrails, dashboards.

  7. Change and adoption lead: runs onboarding training and names business champions.

Evidence kept: tenant record, budget, data-class register entry.

Use-case intake

  1. Requester: submits the problem, users, data needed, expected value and today's baseline KPI.

  2. Solution architect: checks for reusable agents and connectors and proposes a reference pattern.

  3. Chief AI Officer's office: assigns the risk tier.

  4. Approver by tier: business unit head (1), Chief AI Officer (2) or AI council (3) approves, rejects or parks it.

  5. Delivery manager: plans, staffs and sets delivery gates.

  6. PMO: adds it to the portfolio and the benefits tracker.

Onboard a new agent

  1. Agent owner: registers the agent with its Agent Card, owner, use-case ID, tier, data sources, tools and models.

  2. Platform (automated): validates and signs the card, and checks the template, owner and required telemetry.

  3. Data steward: confirms sources and labels; the DPO joins when personal data is involved (tier 2+).

  4. CISO delegate (tier 2+): issues the agent's identity and least-privilege scopes, and reviews threats including prompt injection.

  5. QA: runs the eval suite and red-team cases and attaches the results.

  6. Responsible AI lead and CRO (tier 3): review the evidence pack and approve.

  7. Release manager: raises the change record and runs a canary release.

  8. Platform engineering: publishes the agent to the registry so others can discover it, and switches on monitoring.

Onboard a new model or vendor

  1. Requesting team: makes the business case for the model or provider.

  2. Procurement: sends the vendor questionnaire: data use, training on our data, retention, residency, exit terms.

  3. CISO and DPO (tier 2+): complete the security review and data-processing agreement.

  4. General Counsel and CRO (tier 3 uses): approve contract terms and IP indemnity.

  5. LLMOps: runs the standard eval suite plus cost and latency benchmarks.

  6. Platform engineering: adds the model to the catalogue with its allowed data classes and region, and configures it in the gateway.

Change

Approve a prompt version

  1. Prompt designer or AI engineer: creates a new version in the prompt hub with a change note.

  2. Prompt hub (automated): scans for secrets and personal data, then runs the eval suite against the current production version.

  3. Agent owner: approves if evals meet thresholds; tier 1 versions go live here.

  4. Product manager or a second prompt designer (tier 2+): reviews the diff and sample outputs.

  5. Responsible AI lead (tier 3): reviews behaviour on sensitive and adversarial cases.

  6. Release manager (tier 3): raises a change record; rollback is the previous version's production label.

  7. LLMOps: watches quality and cost for 24–72 hours and rolls back automatically on regression.

Change the model behind an agent

  1. Agent owner or LLMOps: picks a candidate from the model catalogue.

  2. QA: runs regression evals and compares cost and latency.

  3. Data steward: confirms the model is allowed for the agent's data classes and region.

  4. Approvers by tier: owner (1), plus LLMOps and QA (2), plus Responsible AI lead and CRO (3).

  5. Release manager: splits traffic for a canary, with rollback to the old model.

  6. FinOps: is informed of the cost change.

Release to production

  1. Agent owner: bundles the release: agent, prompt versions, tools and model versions.

  2. Pipeline (automated): runs evals, security scans (including AI-generated code) and policy checks.

  3. Release manager (tier 2+): raises the change record and books a calendar slot.

  4. Change advisory board (tier 3): approves.

  5. SRE: watches SLOs during the canary, then promotes or rolls back.

  6. Platform support: publishes release notes and updates the status page.

Access

Grant agent-to-agent access

  1. Calling agent's owner: requests specific skills from the target's Agent Card, stating purpose, data passed and expected volume.

  2. Platform (automated): checks both agents are registered, their cards are signed, and the tiers are compatible.

  3. Target agent's owner: approves the skills and sets rate limits; tier 1 grants end here.

  4. CISO delegate (tier 2+): sets OAuth scopes and the token-exchange rule, so each call carries both the user and the calling agent.

  5. Data owner and DPO (tier 3, or personal data crossing teams): approve the data flow; the Responsible AI lead joins for decisions about people.

  6. Platform engineering: writes the grant into gateway policy with an expiry (for example 90 days) and logs every call.

Grant an agent tool or data access

  1. Agent owner: requests the MCP tool or data source, read or write, and why.

  2. Tool or data owner: approves.

  3. Data steward: checks the source's label against the agent's tier and the models it uses.

  4. CISO and DPO (tier 3, or write access to systems of record): approve.

  5. Platform engineering: issues a scoped, short-lived credential from the vault and adds a human-approval rule for irreversible actions.

Quarterly access review

  1. Platform (automated): lists each owner's agent identities, scopes, A2A grants and permissions unused in 90 days.

  2. Agent owner: recertifies or removes each item; anything not recertified is revoked.

  3. CISO: samples tier 2 and 3 grants.

  4. Internal audit: tests tier 3 controls and reports findings to the CRO.

Run and retire

Budget or quota change

  1. Agent owner: requests more budget with a reason and forecast.

  2. FinOps: checks cost per outcome and whether caching or cheaper-model routing would avoid the increase.

  3. Approver by size: FinOps (small), business unit head (medium), CFO (above the set threshold).

  4. Platform engineering: updates the virtual-key budget and alert thresholds.

Incident and kill switch

  1. Detection: a guardrail, SIEM or SLO alert, or a user report through support.

  2. On-call SRE or security operations: contains it at once by disabling the agent, tool or model, and preserves traces.

  3. Incident commander: sets severity and informs the owner and CISO; adds the CRO, General Counsel and communications for tier 3 or data breaches.

  4. DPO: assesses breach-notification duties under DPDP or GDPR.

  5. Agent owner and QA: find the root cause, fix it and add new eval cases.

  6. Release manager: controls re-enablement; a post-incident review follows.

Personal-data deletion

  1. DPO: receives and verifies the request.

  2. Data steward: finds the person's data across sources, indexes, agent memory and logs, applying any legal retention exemptions.

  3. Platform engineering: deletes or re-embeds, and purges agent memory and caches.

  4. DPO: confirms completion and records the evidence.

Decommission an agent

  1. Trigger: owner request, no use for 90 days, or a policy breach.

  2. Agent owner: warns dependent agents and teams found in the registry's dependency map.

  3. Platform engineering: revokes identity, keys and A2A grants, and removes the agent from discovery.

  4. Data steward: deletes memory and indexes per the retention schedule.

  5. Responsible AI lead (tier 3): confirms required records are kept.

  6. FinOps: closes the budget.
